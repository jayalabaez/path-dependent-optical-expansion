# v7 — Continuous photon-energy-loss falsification

## Question tested

Could cosmological redshift be produced in otherwise static space by a photon continuously losing a fixed fraction of its energy per unit distance?

The minimal model is

\[
\frac{dE}{dr}=-\frac{H_0}{c}E,
\qquad
1+z=\exp\!\left(\frac{H_0r}{c}\right),
\qquad
r(z)=\frac{c}{H_0}\ln(1+z).
\]

The photon speed is kept equal to `c`. This choice deliberately avoids the GW170817 propagation-speed failure that killed the earlier photon-index model. It therefore tests a different and stronger tired-light branch.

## Executed verdict

The pure stationary energy-loss model is **falsified**.

It fails five independent gates:

1. Type Ia supernova time dilation;
2. the physical CMB temperature at redshift `z=0.89`;
3. cosmic distance duality in the pure-loss model;
4. cosmic distance duality even after adding an engineered pulse stretch;
5. radial-versus-transverse BAO geometry.

The code also quantifies spectral-line broadening, local laboratory reach, and future redshift-drift discrimination.

## 1. Supernova time dilation

A pure energy-loss law changes photon energy but does not change the interval between successive emitted pulses. It therefore predicts

\[
\Delta t_{\rm obs}=\Delta t_{\rm emit},
\qquad b=0,
\]

in

\[
\Delta t_{\rm obs}=\Delta t_{\rm emit}(1+z)^b.
\]

The Dark Energy Survey measured

\[
b=1.003\pm0.005_{\rm stat}\pm0.010_{\rm sys}
\]

from 1,504 Type Ia supernovae. Combining the quoted uncertainties in quadrature, the pure-loss prediction is rejected by

\[
89.71\sigma.
\]

Source: White et al., *The Dark Energy Survey Supernova Program: Slow supernovae show cosmological time dilation out to z~1*, arXiv:2406.05050.

An engineered rule that independently stretches pulses by `(1+z)` predicts `b=1` and passes this one test at `0.27 sigma`. It is no longer a pure energy-loss mechanism.

## 2. Physical CMB temperature

A distance-only loss mechanism with a static local radiation bath predicts the CMB temperature at a remote absorber remains

\[
T_0=2.72548\ \mathrm{K}.
\]

ALMA measured

\[
T_{\rm CMB}(z=0.89)=5.13\pm0.06\ \mathrm{K}.
\]

The static-bath prediction is rejected at

\[
40.08\sigma.
\]

The globally evolving law

\[
T(z)=T_0(1+z)
\]

predicts `5.151 K`, only `0.35 sigma` from the measurement. That repair requires the radiation environment itself to evolve with cosmic time; redshift is no longer solely a path-length energy drain.

Source: *A New Precise Measurement of the Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation Temperature at z=0.89 toward PKS1830–211*, DOI: 10.3847/1538-4357/ae0a1e.

## 3. Distance duality

In static Euclidean geometry, `D_A=r`. If photon energy is reduced by `(1+z)^-1` and the arrival rate is reduced by `(1+z)^-b`, then

\[
D_L=r(1+z)^{(1+b)/2}
\]

and

\[
\eta(z)\equiv\frac{D_L}{(1+z)^2D_A}
=(1+z)^{(b-3)/2}.
\]

At `z=1`:

- pure energy loss (`b=0`) gives `eta=0.353553`;
- energy loss plus engineered time dilation (`b=1`) gives `eta=0.5`;
- metric photon-number-conserving propagation gives `eta=1`.

For a diagnostic comparison with the published linear parameterization

\[
\eta(z)=1+\eta_0z,
\qquad \eta_0=0.022\pm0.025,
\]

the two static predictions differ by approximately `26.74 sigma` and `20.88 sigma`, respectively.

This is a single-point equivalent-parameter diagnostic, not a substitute for re-running the full PantheonPlus+BAO covariance likelihood. The qualitative failure is nevertheless large: recent PantheonPlus, DESY5 and DESI DR2 analyses find no evidence for the order-one distance-duality violations required here.

Sources: arXiv:2603.27616; arXiv:2507.13811.

## 4. BAO geometry

For the static loss law,

\[
D_M=\frac{c}{H_0}\ln(1+z),
\qquad
D_H=\frac{c}{H_0(1+z)},
\]

so the Alcock–Paczynski combination is

\[
F_{\rm AP}^{\rm loss}=\frac{D_M}{D_H}
=(1+z)\ln(1+z).
\]

Against a representative flat `Omega_m=0.3` Lambda-CDM geometry, the executed fractional differences are:

| z | fractional difference |
|---:|---:|
| 0.50 | +5.39% |
| 1.00 | +2.07% |
| 2.33 | -11.09% |

DESI DR2 Ly-alpha BAO measures the radial and transverse scales at `z_eff=2.33` at approximately percent precision. A static loss law cannot generally repair both directions by altering only photon energy.

Sources: DESI DR2, arXiv:2503.14738 and arXiv:2503.14739.

## 5. Stochastic collision broadening

Suppose the redshift is generated by `N` independent microscopic loss events. For equal mean logarithmic losses and Poisson event-count fluctuations,

\[
\sigma_{\ln E}=\frac{\ln(1+z)}{\sqrt N}.
\]

Using the conservative resolving scale `1/R` with ESPRESSO `R=140,000`, a `z=1` signal requires

\[
N>9.42\times10^9
\]

and a mean logarithmic loss per event below

\[
7.36\times10^{-11}.
\]

Thus ordinary coarse scattering or collision models would broaden narrow spectral features. This does not falsify a perfectly coherent deterministic map, but it forces any microscopic mechanism into an extreme forward/coherent limit.

Source for instrument resolution: ESO ESPRESSO instrument overview.

## 6. Local tests

The required loss coefficient is

\[
\alpha=\frac{H_0}{c}=7.567\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{m}^{-1}
\]

for `H0=70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1`, corresponding to a loss length of `4.283 Gpc`.

Across one astronomical unit, the predicted fractional shift is only

\[
1.13\times10^{-15}.
\]

This explains why an ordinary laboratory path does not directly test the Hubble-scale coefficient without very long effective optical storage and extraordinary systematic control.

## 7. Redshift drift

For a fixed source, fixed observer and stationary loss coefficient,

\[
\dot z=0.
\]

At `z=3.962`, representative flat Lambda-CDM predicts a velocity drift near

\[
-4.97\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}\,yr^{-1}}.
\]

The 2026 ESPRESSO measurement is

\[
\dot v=-3.5\pm3.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}\,yr^{-1}},
\]

so present precision cannot discriminate the two. This remains a clean future test.

Source: arXiv:2603.02318.

At that point the construction changes clocks, rulers, focusing and radiation together. Operationally it is an evolving effective spacetime metric, not simple photon tired light.
